Post‑Workshop Survey Summary

Key insights & recommendations for future workshop facilitators — based on the three post‑workshop survey results (facilitators: Farihim, Hafiezul, and Sheue Shin) to help prepare more effective sessions.

2026 · 3 workshops · June–July

What worked well

top strengths

1. Clear and effective facilitation

Facilitators were consistently praised for explaining concepts clearly.

  • Clear explanations
  • Easy to understand
  • Informative session
  • Well organized
  • Good introduction to the topic

Presentation and communication are already a strong point across all workshops.

2. Relevant topics

Participants appreciated that topics were closely related to their work:

  • Useful in daily workflow.
  • Topic was relatable.
  • Good exposure to new tools.
  • Very interesting topic.

Practical, real‑world topics increase engagement and perceived value.

3. Positive learning experience

  • Better understanding after attending
  • Improved knowledge and skills
  • Increased confidence
  • Satisfaction with workshop quality
Several respondents described the workshops as a benchmark for future sessions.

Common improvement areas

Although satisfaction was high, recurring suggestions emerged across the surveys.

1 More hands‑on practice most frequent

Participants consistently requested more implementation exercises, practical activities, time to experiment, and guided practice.

  • Need more time.
  • Implementation part could be improved.
  • Hands‑on session could include more activities.
  • Maybe conduct a Version 2 workshop.
Recommendation: reduce lecture time; increase live demonstrations; include step‑by‑step exercises; reserve at least 40–50% of the session for hands‑on activities.

2 Share learning materials after the workshop

Participants requested resources such as workshop slides, reference materials, cheat sheets, and documentation.

  • Share presentation slides.
  • Provide an n8n cheat sheet.
  • Share reference materials for future use.
Recommendation: prepare a resource package — presentation slides, cheat sheet, useful links, sample projects, and FAQs.

3 Improve pre‑workshop preparation

Several participants experienced setup issues. Share prerequisites earlier, specify software versions, list dependencies, and provide setup instructions beforehand.

  • Include Node.js version.
  • Include package dependencies.
  • Allow participants to complete setup beforehand.
Recommendation: send a pre‑workshop checklist 2–3 days in advance — required software, version numbers, installation guide, required accounts, sample files.

4 Match workshop pace to participants

Some participants felt the workshop could allow more time for learning.

Recommendation: slow down during technical demonstrations; pause for questions after each major section; include short recap sessions before moving on. This improves confidence among beginners while keeping the session accessible.

Best practices & future facilitator guide

clear explanations real‑world examples relevant use cases organized agenda interactive demonstrations friendly facilitation practical content for daily work

Successful workshops shared these characteristics — they should remain the foundation of future workshops.

Before the workshop
  • Share prerequisites and installation guide
  • Provide required software versions and dependencies
  • Ensure participants complete setup beforehand
  • Share the workshop agenda in advance
During the workshop
  • Explain concepts clearly
  • Demonstrate practical, real‑world examples
  • Increase interactive and hands‑on activities
  • Allow time for questions and troubleshooting
  • Keep the pace suitable for mixed skill levels
After the workshop
  • Share presentation slides
  • Provide cheat sheets or quick‑reference guides
  • Share sample projects or source code
  • Include useful documentation and learning resources
  • Consider follow‑up or advanced (“Part 2”) workshops
Keep: clear explanations, real‑world use cases, friendly facilitation

Key takeaways

strengths

  • Clear and engaging facilitation
  • Relevant and practical topics
  • Significant improvement in understanding and knowledge
opportunities
  • Increase hands‑on practice and implementation exercises
  • Share post‑workshop learning materials

Implementing interactive shifts — supported by better preparation and accessible materials — will further boost confidence and application.

Overall conclusion

The survey results indicate that the workshops were highly successful in delivering valuable learning experiences, with participants consistently rating them positively. The primary opportunity for future workshops is to shift from information delivery toward more interactive, hands‑on learning, supported by better preparation before the session and accessible reference materials afterward. Implementing these improvements is likely to further enhance participant engagement, confidence, and the practical application of the skills learned.

Next step: adopt the “Before · During · After” framework for all future workshops.
