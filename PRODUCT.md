# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- **L&D owners** who need a reliable, repeatable way to turn post-workshop CSV exports into decision-ready feedback reports.
- **Facilitators** who need fast, practical signals about what to preserve and what to improve before the next session.
- **Leadership** who need concise, evidence-led outcomes, risks, and recommended actions without needing to inspect raw survey rows.
- **Public visitors** who may view intentionally published, aggregated reports but should not encounter raw respondent data by default.

## Product Purpose

Workshop Survey Reports is a static, privacy-conscious reporting tool. It lets users supply compatible Google Forms CSV exports in their browser, generate a clear post-workshop analysis, and download or share the resulting report. It also hosts explicitly published aggregate reports through GitHub Pages.

Success means a non-technical training owner can understand workshop health, identify the most actionable improvement areas, and communicate next steps in minutes—without uploading respondent data to a third-party service.

## Brand Personality

Trusted, analytical, and practical. The experience should feel calm and evidence-led: confident enough for leadership review, direct enough for facilitators, and never flashy or overly technical.

## Anti-references

- Do not resemble a generic SaaS dashboard with decorative KPI cards, gradients, or data theater.
- Do not trade readability for visual novelty, dense tables, or chart clutter.
- Do not imply certainty beyond what small, voluntary survey samples support.
- Do not make raw respondent feedback public by default.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence before emphasis** — show the base size, data limits, and definitions alongside conclusions.
2. **Actions over applause** — use strong scores to identify what to preserve and relative gaps to define the next improvement.
3. **Private by design** — browser-generated reports keep uploaded files on the user’s device; published content is deliberate and aggregated.
4. **One report, two reading speeds** — provide an executive summary first and detailed distributions for people who need to validate it.
5. **Accessible clarity is quality** — legible data, keyboard-first interactions, plain language, and reduced-motion support are baseline requirements.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Use semantic HTML, visible keyboard focus, touch-friendly controls, non-color-only chart cues, sufficient contrast, responsive tables, and a reduced-motion experience. Avoid relying on analytics jargon or statistical notation without plain-language interpretation.
